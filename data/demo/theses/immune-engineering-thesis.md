# Larchmere Immune Ventures: Fund I Investment Thesis

*Sample thesis written to demonstrate the VC Due Diligence Tool. The fund and its people are fictional.*

## Who we are

Larchmere Immune Ventures is a seed-stage fund that builds companies around university immunology research. Fund I is 85 million dollars. We lead seed rounds of 2 to 6 million dollars and usually start the company with the inventors, around an exclusive university license.

## What we invest in

- **Engineered immune cells.** CAR T, TCR and other adoptive cell therapies, and the methods that make transferred cells persist and keep working.
- **Immunomodulators.** Antibodies, cytokines, fusion proteins and small molecules that turn immune responses up (cancer) or down (autoimmune, inflammatory and fibrotic disease).
- **Both directions of the immune system.** We back cancer immunotherapy and autoimmune disease in the same fund, because the biology and the teams overlap.

## Problems we want solved

1. Cell therapies do not yet work in solid tumors: transferred T cells become exhausted, do not persist, and are shut down by the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment.
2. Patients with lupus, systemic sclerosis, inflammatory bowel disease and other immune-driven conditions mostly receive broad systemic immunosuppressants or steroids, with serious side effects.
3. Checkpoint inhibitors help only a minority of patients, so new checkpoints and combinations are needed.

## Stage

We invest from early preclinical work to IND-enabling studies. We need in vivo efficacy in at least one disease model. Ideas with only in vitro data are too early for us.

## Deal structure

We need an exclusive, worldwide license. A US provisional or PCT filing is enough at seed. Patents the university does not fully control, for example ones co-owned with a company, make a deal harder and need a clear path to exclusivity. We want at least one inventor who will work closely with the new company.

## Outside our scope

Diagnostics and laboratory tests, research tools and screening platforms sold to other drug developers, medical devices and imaging, digital health and software, anti-infectives (antibiotics, antifungals and antivirals), animal health, agriculture, and anything outside human therapeutics.
