# Foxglove Electrification Partners: Fund II Investment Thesis

*Sample thesis written to demonstrate the VC Due Diligence Tool. The fund and its people are fictional.*

## Who we are

Foxglove Electrification Partners invests in advanced-materials companies that make electrification cheaper and more reliable. Fund II is 150 million dollars. We lead seed and Series A rounds of 3 to 12 million dollars, often in spinouts built on university materials science.

## What we invest in

- **Energy storage.** Battery chemistries and electrolytes beyond conventional lithium-ion, including sodium and sulfur batteries, and dielectric materials for high-power capacitors.
- **Green hydrogen.** Electrocatalysts and electrodes for water electrolysis made from earth-abundant elements instead of platinum-group metals.
- **Substitutes for critical materials.** Rare-earth-free permanent magnets for motors and generators, and thin-film solar absorbers made from abundant elements.

## Problems we want solved

1. Lithium-ion batteries lose most of their energy in extreme cold, and lithium and cobalt supply is concentrated and expensive.
2. Electrolyzers depend on scarce, costly platinum-group catalysts, and cheaper catalysts are not stable enough.
3. Electric motors depend on neodymium and other rare earths that are mined and processed in very few places.

## Stage

We invest once a material has measured performance against an industry benchmark (for example capacity, cycle life, overpotential or magnetic energy product) and a manufacturing route that could scale. Computational predictions, or results on a single unoptimized sample with no device data, are too early for us.

## Deal structure

We take an exclusive license to filed patent applications, US or PCT, covering the material or how it is made. Provisional-only filings are acceptable only with a plan to convert them within a year. We co-invest with strategic partners from the battery, hydrogen and automotive industries.

## Outside our scope

Carbon removal and carbon-credit business models, software-only products such as grid or energy-management software, cameras and sensors, medical devices, and anything in the life sciences.
